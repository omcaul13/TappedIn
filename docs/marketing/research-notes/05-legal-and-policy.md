# 5. What TappedIn can and can't show in posts and ads (legal and platform policy)

Research date: 2026-10-21. Agent 5 of 8.

> **Not legal advice.** I'm a research agent, not a lawyer. This is practical risk guidance drawn from platform policies, regulations and trade-press legal commentary. Before any paid campaign that names leagues, networks or TV services, a short consult (for example a university law-school IP or entrepreneurship clinic) is worth it.

Labels: **[DATA]** = primary policy, statute, court or registry record. **[CONSENSUS]** = several secondary legal or industry sources agree. **[ANECDOTE]** = a single report or example.

---

## 1. Top findings

1. **Don't put any real broadcast video in posts or ads, even when it's your own screen recording of TappedIn.** The NFL's on-air notice says the telecast is licensed for private use only, and the NFL had a critic's YouTube clip of that very notice taken down [15]. Recording your own legal subscription doesn't change who owns the broadcast. On YouTube, Content ID matching is automatic [10], and YouTube says automated systems can't judge fair use [11]. A "fair use" argument therefore only helps after the claim, through a dispute, and it never prevents one. [DATA]
2. **Strikes add up to account loss.** On YouTube, 3 copyright strikes within 90 days can get a channel terminated [12]. US law requires platforms to have a repeat-infringer termination policy to keep DMCA safe harbor [13]. The UFC, NBA and NFL have asked the USPTO to define "expeditious" removal of live content as "near-instantaneous" [14]. A brand-new account can't afford even one strike. [DATA]
3. **Every major ad platform has a rule a streaming app could trip.** Google Ads bans "unauthorized sites or software" that "capture, copy, or provide access to" copyrighted content, including TV shows, and bans DRM circumvention tools [1]. Its "Enabling dishonest behavior" policy lists "stealing cable" and VPNs marketed to get around geo-restrictions [2]. TikTok Ads bans clips "from any unauthorized media sources" and any third-party brand used without authorization (policy updated April 2026) [7]. Meta lists pirated broadcasts under prohibited piracy [6]. TappedIn does none of these things, but automated reviewers judge the creative and the landing page, so the ad has to *read* as legitimate. [DATA]
4. **Google also checks software-download ads under its Unwanted Software policy.** The ad or landing page must say what the product is (app, extension, software) and what it does, link to terms or a EULA, and not make it hard to uninstall [4]. The download page needs a plain "What it does / What it needs / How to uninstall" block. [DATA]
5. **Plain-text compatibility statements are the defensible way to name a TV service. Logos are not.** Nominative fair use (the *New Kids on the Block* test) allows a trademark when (a) the product can't be identified without it, (b) you use only as much of the mark as needed, and (c) nothing suggests sponsorship [18]. "Works with your Xfinity On Campus login" in plain text fits. A logo uses more of the mark than needed and suggests partnership. This supports the project's existing no-logo decision. [CONSENSUS]
6. **Platforms are stricter than trademark law.** TikTok's ad policy forbids third-party brands without authorization, with no carve-out for compatibility statements [7]. Google allows a trademark in ad text unless the owner complains; after a complaint, the restriction covers the whole domain going forward [3]. Practical rule: keep service, network and league names out of ad headlines and visuals, and name services on the landing page and in organic posts. [DATA]
7. **"Red zone" changed hands in 2026, so be careful with it.** ESPN closed its NFL Network deal in Feb 2026 and got RedZone distribution and brand rights. Press reports say ESPN could launch RedZone channels for college football [22]. "RED ZONE CHANNEL" is registered for sports TV services (Reg. 3182102, now owned by ESPN, on the Supplemental Register, renewal due Dec 2026) [19]. The NFL filed "REDZONE" in May 2025 [21], while the older NFL REDZONE registration was cancelled in 2021 [20]. Lowercase "red zone" in football commentary is generic. "RedZone for college football" or "a RedZone channel" as a product pitch invites a confusion claim. [DATA]
8. **NCAA marks are off-limits in commercial posts and ads.** The NCAA's Advertising and Promotional Guidelines (effective July 1, 2026) bar unauthorized use of March Madness, The Big Dance, Final Four and championship names, plus "ambush marketing" [23]. A Feb 2025 NCAA memo says the marks may not appear on websites or social media without authorization [24]. Trade guidance suggests generic wording like "tournament time" [25]. [DATA]
9. **Don't say "Super Bowl" in marketing, but "the big game" is widely used.** The NFL sends cease-and-desist letters over unlicensed "Super Bowl" ad copy, and disclaimers don't stop them. The NFL abandoned its "THE BIG GAME" application after opposition, and the author knows of no reported NFL claims over that phrase (Jan 2026) [26]. [CONSENSUS]
10. **School marks: Northeastern's own policy applies to the founders.** NU Policy 116 (revised May 20, 2026) bans unauthorized use of the university's name, logo or other identifiers for marketing, and the university doesn't endorse commercial products [27]. Other schools license their marks through CLC or Learfield and require a license for any use in advertising [28]. Don't use school logos, colors-as-branding or "the official app for [school]." "Built by two Northeastern students" is a factual bio line, but avoid it in paid ads unless NU Marketing approves. [DATA]
11. **Founders, friends and ambassadors must disclose.** The FTC Endorsement Guides (revised July 2023) require a "clear and conspicuous" disclosure of any material connection, meaning one that is "unavoidable" on social media. A profile-only disclosure, or one behind a "more" link, fails, and platform tools alone may not be enough [29]. Free products, gift cards, merch, family and employment ties all count [30]. [DATA]
12. **The FTC's Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule (16 CFR 465, in force since Oct 21, 2024) covers astroturfing.** It bans fake reviews, reviews by officers or managers, undisclosed insider reviews, incentives conditioned on positive sentiment, and buying fake followers or views. The FTC can seek civil penalties of about $51,744 or more per violation (inflation-adjusted) [31]. [DATA]
13. **Brand accounts need cleared music.** TikTok business accounts can't use the general library commercially and must use the Commercial Music Library (about 1M tracks, updated July 2025) [8]. Instagram and Facebook business accounts are steered to Meta Sound Collection, and Meta's ad policy requires licenses for music in ads [5][9]. Trending chart audio is generally not cleared for brand posts. [DATA/CONSENSUS]
14. **"Skip commercials" messaging has a real precedent.** Fox sued DISH over the AutoHop ad-skipping feature in 2012. DISH largely won on copyright, but Fox won breach-of-contract findings and the case settled in 2016 [17]. In *ABC v. Aereo* (2014), the Supreme Court sided with broadcasters against a service that retransmitted TV without a license [16]. Networks react badly to products sold as defeating ads. TappedIn doesn't remove ads, so don't market it as if it does. [DATA]
15. **TV services' terms restrict automation.** The Xfinity Web Services Terms (effective Feb 13, 2024) say you won't access the services "through any automated means, including scripts" (except APIs provided for that purpose), and won't let others use your credentials [32]. YouTube's terms ban automated access without written permission [33]. Marketing should never encourage account sharing, password sharing or "using your friend's login." [DATA]

## 3. Post and ad checklist (answer yes to all before posting)

1. Is there **zero** real broadcast video or audio (including commentators and in-stadium PA) anywhere in the frame or soundtrack?
2. Are all screens showing TappedIn **replay mode / data tiles / mock or blurred video** only?
3. Are there **no** team, league, network, TV-service or school logos (including on clothing in the shot)?
4. Is every TV-service name **plain text**, used only to say what it works with, and paired with "not affiliated…"?
5. Does it avoid **March Madness, Final Four, Big Dance, Super Bowl, RedZone** and other league marks?
6. Does it avoid **free-streams, bypass, hack, unlock, skip ads, no cable needed, official** language?
7. Does it make clear (or at least not contradict) that **your own TV subscription** is required?
8. Is the music from the **TikTok CML, Meta Sound Collection, licensed stock or original**?
9. If anyone in it got anything of value (money, gift card, merch, early access, contest entry), is there a **#ad or "paid partnership" disclosure in the first line, on screen and spoken**, with the platform's toggle on?
10. If a founder is posting, does the post **say they built it**?
11. Are all reviews and testimonials **real, from real users, unedited in meaning, and unpaid** (or disclosed)?
12. Does it avoid encouraging **account or password sharing** or automation beyond what the app does?
13. For ads: does the landing page say **what the software does, what it needs, link the terms, and explain how to uninstall**?
14. Does every person shown on camera have a **signed (even informal) release**?
15. Is no **NU (or other school) name or logo** used as an endorsement, beyond a factual bio line?
16. Would a reviewer who's never heard of TappedIn come away thinking **"legit app for my own TV service"** and not "piracy tool"?

## 5. Disclosure rules summary (creators, ambassadors, friends, founders)

- **Who must disclose:** anyone with a material connection: paid creators, ambassadors paid in cash, **gift cards, merch, swag or early perks**, contest entrants rewarded for posting, friends or family asked to post, and the founders themselves [29][30].
- **How:** in the post itself, "unavoidable." Put it in the first line of the caption, **on screen and spoken in video**, and repeat it periodically in live streams. "#ad," "#sponsored," "Paid partnership with TappedIn," or "I built this app." Avoid vague tags ("#collab," "#ambassador," "#sp") and burying it in hashtag piles, behind "more," or only in the bio [29][30].
- **Platform tools:** turn on TikTok's content-disclosure toggle ("Paid partnership" for creators; the "Your brand" option for founders promoting their own app) [34], and use Meta's Paid Partnership label (the brand approves the tag; required when the creator got anything of value) [35]. These are **in addition to**, not instead of, the FTC disclosure [29].
- **Founders:** saying "my app" or "we built this" in the video is the disclosure. Don't post fake "just found this app" content from alt accounts [29][31].
- **Reviews:** no reviews by the founders; no reviews conditioned on being positive; no buying followers, views or comments. Friends and roommates who review must disclose the connection [31].
- **TappedIn's responsibility:** give each ambassador a one-page brief (required disclosure, banned words, no broadcast footage, CML music), check their posts, and stop working with anyone who repeatedly doesn't disclose. The FTC expects advertisers to monitor their endorsers [29].

## 6. Safe content ideas

- Replay-mode screen captures (data tiles, abbreviation badges, blurred highlight reel) with founder voiceover.
- The app's own UI: the switch animation, the decision "reasons," the game picker, settings and setup screens.
- Founders on camera explaining the idea; build-in-public clips (code, testing, the "measured results" numbers).
- Dorm or living-room setup shots with TVs showing TappedIn data tiles or blurred content.
- Animated explainers (motion graphics of "game goes to break → switch → back home").
- Licensed stock footage of fans reacting (no broadcast on screen); reaction shots of real friends watching (screens out of frame).
- Text-on-screen memes about commercials and timeouts that don't use league marks or broadcast imagery.
