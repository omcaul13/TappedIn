> Raw notes from one research agent, 2026-10-21. Some claims here were corrected by the fact-check in [07-fact-check.md](07-fact-check.md); [advertising-research.md](../advertising-research.md) uses the corrected versions.

# 1. Short-form content (TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts) for TappedIn

Research date: 2026-10-21. Labels: [DATA] backed by data, [CONSENSUS] expert or platform consensus, [ANECDOTE] anecdote or opinion. Source numbers refer to section 6.

## 1. Top findings

1. **All three platforms rank short videos mainly on how viewers react, not on follower count.** TikTok uses interactions such as finishing a longer video, shares, comments and follows, and says follower count is not a direct factor [1]. YouTube Shorts uses the share of people who chose to watch, average view duration and percent viewed, plus likes and surveys [7]. Instagram scores Reels on how likely you are to reshare, watch to the end, like, or tap the audio [4]. So a brand-new account like TappedIn's can reach people on day one. [CONSENSUS, from platform statements]
2. **On Instagram, "sends" (shares by DM) are the signal that reaches non-followers.** Mosseri's January 2025 explanation named watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach, with sends mattering most for unconnected reach [5]. The often-quoted "sends count 3–5x likes" figure has no traceable primary source. [CONSENSUS on the signal; weak evidence on the multiplier]
3. **Instagram penalizes unoriginal content.** Watermarked (for example, reposted TikToks), low-quality, silent, framed, mostly-text or already-posted Reels get less reach [4]. In April 2026 the originality rules were extended to accounts that mostly repost other people's work [6]. Make native edits per platform, never cross-post with the TikTok watermark. [CONSENSUS, platform statement]
4. **Hooks: TikTok's ad guidance says to state your proposition within 3 s and land the hook within the first 6 s.** It also suggests 5–10 words per second of on-screen text, a DIY look over a polished one, and real people on camera [2]. Meta says to put key messaging in the first 3 s and cast real people [11]. TikTok publishes no data with these rules. The "63% of top ads hook in 3 s" figure in blogs could not be traced to TikTok. [CONSENSUS]
5. **On TikTok, longer videos get more reach on average.** In Buffer's 1.1M-video analysis (March 2025), videos over 60 s got about 43% more reach and 64% more watch time than shorter ones, but only about 12% of videos run that long, and the data is correlational [13]. Very short clips still go viral. [DATA, vendor dataset]
6. **Posting frequency is a lottery-ticket game.** In Buffer's 11.4M-post TikTok study (October 2025, account-fixed-effects regressions), median views stayed around 500 at every frequency. Going from 1 to 2–5 posts a week raised views per post up to 17%, mostly by making a breakout hit more likely [14]. YouTube says Shorts have "no minimum posting cadence" [7]. [DATA]
7. **"Best time to post" studies conflict, and each covers only its own tool's users.** Buffer's 7.1M-post study (September 2026) found Sunday 9 a.m. best by median engagement rate, while Sprout and others find Tuesday–Thursday afternoons [15]. Use TikTok Studio's "most active times" for your own audience instead. For a sports product, timing should follow the sports calendar (game windows), not generic studies. [DATA, but low value]
8. **Trending sounds help discovery but aren't required.** YouTube says Shorts don't need trending sounds and original content can succeed [8]. TikTok business accounts are limited to the Commercial Music Library, and TikTok's terms bar using general sounds to promote a brand without rights [16]. Instagram blocks boosting Reels that use copyrighted music [20]. **Use a personal creator account for organic posts, but promotional posts still fall under the commercial music rules.** Original voiceover plus screen audio is the safest default. [CONSENSUS]
9. **YouTube Shorts can now be 3 min, but a Short over 1 min with any Content ID claim is blocked globally** [9]. Shorts views have counted every play since 31 March 2025, and the old metric is now called "engaged views" [10]. **This matters a lot for TappedIn: live TV broadcast footage in a video is a Content ID / rights-match risk on every platform.** Replay-mode data tiles avoid that risk. [CONSENSUS, platform statement]
10. **Paid creative should look like organic posts.** TikTok's own (2020–21) internal data says Spark Ads, which boost organic posts, get about 30% higher completion and 142% higher engagement than non-Spark in-feed ads [17]. Meta reports partnership ads (run through a creator's handle) cost 19% less per acquisition, via trade press [18]. YouTube launched Shorts-only partnership ads in 2025 and claims up to 20% more conversions [19]. Google's study of 2,000+ Shorts ads found ads that look like conventional ads get swiped away [12]. [DATA, all vendor-reported]
11. **Sports fans already use TikTok as a second screen.** In a TikTok-commissioned Ipsos study (fieldwork mid-2025, 18–49-year-olds in 7 markets), 85% of fans said they use TikTok during live events and 94% use it for highlights they missed [21]. A TikTok guide says 57% of US users watch sports content weekly [22]. [DATA, but sponsored by the platform]
12. **College-age reach: YouTube 95%, Instagram 80%, TikTok 63% of US adults 18–29** (Pew, survey February–June 2025) [23]. In 2025–2026 TikTok added Campus Verification (6,000 US schools) and Campus Hub group chats, so TikTok is actively building college-specific feeds [24]. [DATA]
13. **Indie-app success stories mostly come from simple phone-filmed demos or paid creators, not polished ads.** Dumbphone grew through handheld phone videos of the app: a reported 2.4M views and 20k downloads in 78 days [25]. Locket (built by a recent UCSB grad) hit #1 on the App Store after a TikTok demo went viral [26]. Cal AI (teen founders) scaled with ~250 paid creators, a big-budget tactic [27]. [ANECDOTE]
14. **Platform changes in the last 18 months:**
    - TikTok US moved to a US joint venture in January 2026, with a mandate to retrain the recommendation algorithm on US data. No confirmed changes for creators so far [28].
    - Instagram added "Your Algorithm" topic controls (test October 2025, expanded June 2026) [29], 3-minute Reels (January 2025) [30] and Trial Reels shown only to non-followers [31].
    - Some Shorts creators reported view drops from September 2025. YouTube has not explained them [32].
    - [CONSENSUS / ANECDOTE mix]

## 2. Ranked content types for TappedIn

1. **"I watched 4 games at once and the app picked the best one during commercials" (screen demo plus face or voice).** One continuous take of the 4-up window: a screen hits a break, swaps to a red-zone drive, then swaps back. This shows the product's single "aha" in under 10 s, which matches TikTok's advice to state the proposition within 3 s [2]. This is the Dumbphone/Locket pattern: the demo is the content [25][26]. Risk: real broadcast video can draw rights claims [9]. Safest options are replay-mode data tiles, or very short real footage with your own voiceover. Evidence: [CONSENSUS + ANECDOTE].
2. **Game-day reaction / "live from the dorm" (people first, product in background).** Students reacting to a big moment, with the app visibly catching it on another screen. This fits TikTok's "Reali-TEA" (unpolished, relatable) trend theme for 2026 [33], and the second-screen behavior in [21]. It posts on high-traffic game days and is shareable to friends, which helps Instagram reach through sends [5]. Evidence: [CONSENSUS, sponsored data].
3. **Dorm / battlestation setup tour ("my Saturday setup").** A familiar, high-curiosity format, with TappedIn as the hero on the monitor. It reaches the campus-TV audience directly, but build-your-setup content tends to drive saves more than installs. Evidence: [ANECDOTE].
4. **POV / skit memes ("POV: your game goes to commercial for the 4th time this drive").** Very shareable on Instagram (sends) [5]. End on the product shot. It reaches people well, but the link to installs is weaker unless the product appears early. Evidence: [ANECDOTE].
5. **Build-in-public / "two college students built this".** A personal story attracts tech and student viewers and gives creators and press a story to cover. Good for Shorts and Reels, and for recruiting beta testers. On its own it rarely drives installs among non-technical fans. Evidence: [ANECDOTE] [25][34].
6. **2-minute setup tutorial.** It gets fewer views but converts people who are already interested. Pin it, and use it as the reply-to-comment video for "how do I get this?" questions. Evidence: [CONSENSUS].
7. **Creator-led content (sports and college creators).** This is the best route to installs on paid budgets, run through Spark / partnership ads [17][18][19]. Cal AI's scale (hundreds of creators on retainer) is a big-budget tactic [27]. At $100–500/month, one or two micro-creators or campus fans is realistic. Evidence: [DATA, vendor].
8. **Green-screen commentary** (over the scoreboard or a news headline about blackouts or channel prices). Cheap and timely, but it carries mostly-text and originality risks on Instagram [4]. Use it sparingly. [ANECDOTE]

## 3. Platform notes

### TikTok
- **Length:** Over 60 s correlates with more reach [13], but a 10–20 s demo is the right first test. Text overlays: 5–10 words per second, inside the safe zone [2].
- **Hook:** The proposition within 3 s, the hook within 6 s [2]. Show 4 screens in the first frame, with text like "it switches when your game goes to commercial."
- **Audio:** A personal or creator account can use trending sounds for non-promotional posts. Business accounts and promotional posts must use the Commercial Music Library [16]. Original voiceover is the safest choice.
- **Frequency:** 2–5 posts a week is the efficient level [14]. Post during game windows (Saturday for college football, Sunday for NFL, weeknights for NBA/NHL).
- **Organic vs paid:**
  - Spark Ads boost an existing organic or creator post, so the post keeps its profile, comments and likes [17].
  - Promote (in-app boost) has a "More website visits" goal [35]. The ~$3/day minimum is from third parties and unverified.
  - **$0:** organic only, with an experiment budget of ~3 posts a week.
  - **$100/month:** Spark or Promote your one best organic post per week (after it proves itself organically).
  - **$500/month:** add one micro-creator post a month and run it as a Spark Ad.
- **Note:** TikTok's "Campus" features [24] suggest college-specific feeds are growing.

### Instagram Reels
- **Length:** Up to 3 min [30]. Keep demos short, because watch time is a core signal [5].
- **Hook:** Key message in the first 3 s, real people on camera [11]. Meta suggests keeping about 14% at the top, 35% at the bottom and 6% at each side clear of text [11].
- **Audio:** Reels with audio get more delivery. Meta reports over 75% of Reels views are sound-on, and music plus voiceover scores better [11][36]. Reels with copyrighted music can't be boosted [20].
- **Originality:** No TikTok watermark, no reposts [4][6]. Use **Trial Reels** to test hooks on non-followers without filling your grid [31].
- **Frequency:** Buffer's Instagram data (secondary report) suggests 3–5 posts a week for follower growth. That is unverified here.
- **Organic vs paid:**
  - Boost (simple) or Ads Manager.
  - Partnership ads run through a creator's or a student fan's handle [18].
  - **$100/month:** boost the best Reel to 18–24-year-olds near target campuses.
  - **$500/month:** partnership ads with 1–2 creators.

### YouTube Shorts
- **Length:** Up to 3 min, but **keep any Short with outside audio or footage under 60 s**, because a claim blocks longer ones [9].
- **Hook:** YouTube's "viewed vs swiped away" metric measures the hook directly [7]. Creator blogs suggest a target of 70–80%, which is not an official benchmark.
- **Audio:** Trending sounds are optional [8]. Shorts Audio Library tracks are fine up to 1 min [9].
- **Frequency:** "No minimum cadence" [7]. Upload the same edits as TikTok (without the watermark). This costs little extra effort and reaches the biggest 18–29 audience [23].
- **Organic vs paid:**
  - Shorts ads run through Google Ads video/Demand Gen campaigns.
  - Partnership ads (2025) co-brand a creator's Short and can point to a download [19].
  - **Budget:** treat Shorts as organic-only below $500/month.
- **Search:** YouTube is also a search engine. Titles like "watch multiple football games at once on PC" can keep getting found over time [7].

## 4. Examples (described and linked)
- **Dumbphone (solo indie dev, 2024):** Handheld phone videos of a black-and-white home-screen app on @dumbphoneapp. Reported 2.4M views, 20k downloads and $9k monthly recurring revenue in 78 days. The writeup is by Shortimize, which sells analytics [25]. https://www.tiktok.com/@dumbphoneapp
- **Locket Widget (2022):** Built by a 22-year-old recent UCSB grad for his long-distance girlfriend. TikTok demos of the widget drove it to #1 on the US App Store, with roughly 1.5M early downloads per Appfigures [26].
- **Cal AI (2023–25):** High-school founders. Growth came from paid creators on TikTok and Instagram (about 250 on retainer, mid-six-figure monthly spend), reaching more than $30M in revenue before a MyFitnessPal acquisition [27]. This shows the creator model scaling, but it is not a $0 playbook.
- **LifePilot build-in-public log (Indie Hackers):** Early videos got about 250 views each, and 7–8 s clips did best. A realistic picture of weeks 1–2 [34].
- **Culture Media "College Tour" (August 2025):** A creator house that follows college football game days and posts about 100 clips a day. It shows how much game-day volume the space produces [37].
- **Texas lineman's multi-TV Saturday (news, 2025):** Two TVs, one on YouTube TV 4-up multiview, "five games at once." The 4-games-at-once desire is real and mainstream. Note that YouTube TV multiview is the direct comparison viewers will make [38].

## 5. Plausible but weak
- **"Sends are worth 3–5x likes" on Instagram:** the source is unclear [5].
- **"63% / 71% of top TikTok ads hook in 3 s":** could not be traced to TikTok [2].
- **Fixed "best time to post" charts:** each study covers only its tool's users and they contradict each other [15].
- **"Bottom 35% safe zone violations cut CTR 28%":** a single vendor claim. The 35% guide itself is real [11].
- **"Shorts algorithm loves 30–45 s":** no source found.
- **"TikTok gives every video 200 test viewers":** folklore. Also, YouTube creator analysts claim Shorts now skips initial test audiences [32].
- **"Mosseri memo: real human content gets 40–60% more distribution":** unsourced.
- **Engagement-rate comparisons across platforms** (TikTok 2.5–5% vs Instagram ~0.5%): vendors calculate them differently, so don't compare them directly [3].
- **Slideshow / "faceless" app-marketing playbooks:** promoted mostly by tool vendors with no install data.
